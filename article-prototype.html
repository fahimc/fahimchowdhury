The prototype is not the point. The learning is.

Use a small, imperfect experiment to find the shape of a useful AI product.

FC Fahim Chowdhury · January 11, 2024
learn
in public

AI prototypes can be persuasive. A smooth demo compresses weeks of uncertainty into a few minutes of possibility.

That is exactly why a prototype should be treated as a question, not an answer. Its job is to make an assumption visible enough for someone to challenge.

Make the unknown smaller

Pick one user, one moment, and one behavior you want to understand. Build only enough to create a real reaction. Then listen carefully to what people do, not just what they say.

The learning compounds when the experiment stays small. You can change the prompt, the interface, or the premise without protecting a large investment.

The fastest path to clarity is a thing people can react to.
